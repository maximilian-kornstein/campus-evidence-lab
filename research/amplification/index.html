Supply or Demand?

Auditing algorithmic amplification of antisemitic content

View PDF ↗ Download PDF

Abstract

Claims that recommendation algorithms amplify antisemitic content rarely say “compared with what”, so they are hard to check. This paper defines amplification as exposure to antisemitic content under the algorithm relative to a named alternative feed, for the same user interests, and shows what randomised test-account audits can and cannot identify. It corrects for detector errors that differ between feeds and gives a stopping rule that lets an audit end as soon as the evidence is clear. It is a methods paper: its results come from simulations on a synthetic recommender, and it reports no real-world findings. An audit protocol with ethical safeguards commits only to a pilot and a feasibility gate.

Methods

  • Amplification as a contrast at fixed demand
  • A Markov model of sessions and exposure
  • Randomised test-account audits
  • Correcting detector errors separately by feed
  • Anytime-valid sequential stopping

What the simulations show

  • In simulated data, detector errors that differed between the two feeds turned a true ratio of 0.89 into a naive estimate of 1.45; correcting each feed separately fixed this, while a single pooled correction made it worse.
  • In simulated audits, the planned stopping rule kept its error rates within their stated limits despite skewed data.
  • Under illustrative inputs, an audit powered to detect a material level of amplification for four user profiles would need about 4,900 test accounts, so the protocol commits only to a pilot and a feasibility gate.
