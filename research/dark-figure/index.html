The Dark Figure

Estimating unreported antisemitic hate crime

View PDF ↗ Download PDF

Abstract

Counts of antisemitic incidents are always partial: police statistics, NGO audits and community reporting channels each record only some of the same incidents. This paper develops a framework for estimating the dark figure, the incidents that appear on no list, by linking records across lists and analysing their overlap with multiple-systems estimation. One key assumption cannot be tested from the data, so the paper treats it as an explicit sensitivity parameter and reports the range of estimates it allows. It is a methods paper: its results come from simulations on synthetic data, and it reports no real-world findings. It also sets out an analysis plan and an ethics protocol for a future study of real records.

Methods

  • Multiple-systems estimation across overlapping lists
  • Sensitivity bounds for an untestable assumption
  • Referral between lists and provenance data
  • Record linkage and the propagation of linkage error
  • A reporting funnel that locates where incidents are lost

What the simulations show

  • In simulated data, when one list copied records from another, the classic two-list estimate was about 39% too low; using each record’s provenance cut the error to about 1%.
  • In simulated data, the sensitivity ranges usually contained the true number of missed incidents, but were often too wide to guide policy without outside information.
  • In simulated data with realistic record errors, the stylised linkage correction overcorrected (about 47% too high, against 40% uncorrected), so the paper uses it only as a diagnostic.
