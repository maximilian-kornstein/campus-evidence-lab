Where Does Criticism End?

A latent-variable map of antisemitic attitudes

View PDF ↗ Download PDF

Abstract

Three widely cited definitions of antisemitism (the IHRA working definition, the Jerusalem Declaration on Antisemitism and the Nexus Document) draw the line between criticism of Israel and antisemitism in different places, and each rests partly on empirical claims about how attitudes fit together. This paper develops a survey measurement tool that tests those claims without choosing between the definitions. For each Israel-related statement, a bifactor model estimates how much it shares with classic stereotypes about Jews and reports it as in, out or undetermined against a threshold fixed in advance. It is a methods paper: its results come from simulations on synthetic survey data, and it reports no real-world findings. The tool is designed to be equally usable, and equally checkable, by people on every side of the definitions debate.

Methods

  • A bifactor graded response model
  • Statement-level estimates against a pre-set threshold
  • Tests for groups reading statements differently
  • List experiments for sensitive questions
  • Small-area estimation on the latent trait

What the simulations show

  • In simulated data with six Israel-related statements and 3,000 respondents, no statement was placed on the wrong side of the threshold; statements close to it were more often reported as undetermined.
  • In simulated data with too few or too similar statements, the map could not be pinned down, yet fits still converged; agreement across starting values exposed only one of three such designs, while an added diagnostic flagged all three.
  • In simulated data, a tendency to agree with any statement, and Israel statements that echo classic tropes, moved some results up and others down unless modelled.
  • In simulated data, the draft plan’s screen for items that groups read differently left measurable bias, so the plan was revised before registration.
