Shock and Contagion

Separating outside shocks from contagion in incident surges

View PDF ↗ Download PDF

Abstract

After a salient outside event, a surge in antisemitic incidents may be a direct reaction to the event, incidents prompting further incidents, or both. This paper models incident streams as a self-exciting (Hawkes) point process with outside shocks, splits a surge into background, direct and contagion parts, and shows when the data cannot tell contagion apart from unrecorded shocks. It also corrects for under-reporting and gives an alert rule that makes the trade-off between false alarms and missed surges explicit. It is a methods paper: its results come from simulations on synthetic data, and it reports no real-world findings.

Methods

  • Multivariate Hawkes processes with outside shocks
  • When contagion can be told apart from shocks
  • Splitting surges into background, direct response and contagion
  • Correction for under-reporting
  • Forecasts and an explicit alert rule

What the simulations show

  • In simulated data with no contagion at all, a model with a constant background rate attributed up to 63% of incidents to contagion; supplying the dates of outside events largely removed this artefact where tested.
  • In simulated data, the under-reporting correction restored accurate uncertainty ranges unless contagion was weak and recording poor; a 30–50% error in the recording rate set a floor on precision that more data could not lower.
  • In simulated data, alerts caught 89% of surges with no new outside event after the forecast, but only 28% of those with one: no model can time new events.
