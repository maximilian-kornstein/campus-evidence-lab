# Institutional Accountability Standard

Campus Evidence Lab uses accountability to mean institution-level transparency from public evidence. The project documents what public records say, how an institution responded in public, what remains unresolved, and how a correction or right-of-reply can be submitted.

This standard does not create rankings, safety scores, prevalence estimates, severity scores, legal findings, or complete incident histories.

## Accountability Commitments

- Identify the institution connected to each source-backed record.
- Show the public source basis and source-family classification for the record.
- Separate imported public-source records from records that have source-family checks, internal certification, or external review.
- Track public institutional responses when those responses are available in the source record.
- Keep unresolved limits visible instead of smoothing them away.
- Provide correction and right-of-reply paths on public pages.

## Permitted Accountability Claims

Campus Evidence Lab may say that an institution has records in the current dataset when those records are linked to public sources and pass publication gates.

Campus Evidence Lab may summarize counts by review tier, source family, category, date range, public response depth, and open review needs.

Campus Evidence Lab may describe whether the public record includes an institutional response, agency-described institutional action, limited response note, or no public response found.

## Prohibited Accountability Claims

Campus Evidence Lab must not claim that a dossier proves legal liability, ranks institutional quality, measures campus safety, estimates incident prevalence, or fully captures all incidents at an institution.

Campus Evidence Lab must not describe bulk-imported records as individually human-certified unless the record has the appropriate review tier and supporting review artifact.

Campus Evidence Lab must not treat a lack of recorded public response as proof that an institution failed to respond outside the available public sources.

## Dossier Minimums

An institution dossier must show:

- record count in the current dataset;
- review-tier mix;
- source-family mix;
- public response-depth counts;
- latest update date;
- citation packet links;
- correction or right-of-reply link;
- a clear use limit against ranking, safety scoring, prevalence measurement, or legal conclusions.

## Right Of Reply

Institutions may submit source-backed corrections, missing public responses, source locators, duplicate reports, and contextual public documents through the public correction pathway.

Accepted corrections update the relevant record, source, or dossier and keep a public changelog entry when a public record changes.
