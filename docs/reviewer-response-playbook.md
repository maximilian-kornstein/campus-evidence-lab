# Reviewer Response Playbook

Use this file once someone replies. The goal is to convert interest into a documented, narrow review without sounding needy or vague.

## General Rule

Always move the conversation toward one concrete review object:

- one page
- one memo
- one guide
- 10 records

Do not keep the conversation broad unless the reviewer chooses that.

## Case 1: They say yes, willing to review

Reply:

```text
Thank you. The narrowest useful option is either:

1. a one-page methodology review, or
2. a 10-record sample review.

If helpful, I can send the 10-record packet and three exact questions so the review stays small.
```

Then send:

- reviewer brief
- 10-record packet link
- 3 narrow questions

## Case 2: They seem interested but busy

Reply:

```text
Understood. Even one sentence on what feels most risky, overstated, or weakest methodologically would be useful.

If easier, the reviewer brief is here:
https://maximilian-kornstein.github.io/campus-evidence-lab/reviewer-brief/
```

Goal:

- reduce burden
- still get a documented critique

## Case 3: They ask for more context

Reply:

```text
Here is the shortest version:

Campus Evidence Lab is a public-source archive of campus civil-rights records. It is designed to help people inspect documented public records without turning them into rumor, rankings, safety scores, or prevalence claims.

The current reviewer brief is here:
https://maximilian-kornstein.github.io/campus-evidence-lab/reviewer-brief/

If useful, I can also send a 10-record sample and three narrow review questions.
```

## Case 4: They ask what exactly you want reviewed

Reply:

```text
The highest-value review targets are:

1. methodology language
2. source standards
3. no-ranking / responsible-use language
4. a 10-record sample for category, source-fit, and overclaiming risk

The most useful output would be what should be stricter, narrower, or more clearly limited.
```

## Case 5: They ask for a packet

Reply:

```text
Thank you. Here is the reviewer brief:
https://maximilian-kornstein.github.io/campus-evidence-lab/reviewer-brief/

And here is a suggested 10-record sample packet:
https://maximilian-kornstein.github.io/campus-evidence-lab/research-workspace/?record_ids=evt_2026_0027,evt_2026_0081,evt_2026_0125,evt_2026_0210,evt_2026_0300,evt_2026_0395,evt_2025_0030,evt_2025_0060,evt_2024_0003,evt_2024_0006

Three narrow questions:
1. What feels weakest or overstated?
2. What should be stricter before the archive grows?
3. What would make this more useful without increasing misuse risk?
```

## Case 6: They offer a call

Reply:

```text
Thank you. I would be glad to do a short call.

The most useful discussion would be:
- what the archive should not claim
- what part of the methodology is weakest
- what a serious outside reviewer would want tightened first

I can also send the reviewer brief beforehand if that helps.
```

After the call:

- log the date
- log scope discussed
- log key feedback
- do not publicly characterize it as endorsement

## Case 7: They give criticism

Reply:

```text
Thank you. This is exactly the kind of feedback I was hoping for.

I’m going to review these points carefully and tighten the project accordingly. If I make changes based on this, I will keep the public language limited to the scope you actually reviewed.
```

Then:

- log the criticism
- map it to a site or methodology change
- record the improvement in the updates page or changelog as appropriate

## Case 8: They decline

Reply:

```text
Understood. Thank you for considering it.

If there is a better person or team for a narrow methodology or source-audit review, I would be grateful for a redirect.
```

If no redirect, stop.

## Case 9: They redirect you internally

Reply:

```text
Thank you. I appreciate the redirect.

I’ll keep the ask narrow: one page or 10 sample records, with the goal of getting critical feedback rather than endorsement.
```

Then send the smaller ask to the new contact.

## Case 10: They say something positive

Do not jump to “Can I quote that?” immediately unless it is clear and substantive.

First reply:

```text
Thank you. I appreciate that.

If you have any criticism or cautions as well, that would be especially useful while the project is still early.
```

Only after there is real review or a meaningful comment should you ask:

```text
Would you be comfortable with me listing your name, organization, or a short quoted line on the acknowledgments page? Only the exact scope reviewed would be described, and only with your permission.
```

## Case 11: They ask whether this is endorsement or partnership

Reply:

```text
No. I’m asking for narrow critical review, not endorsement.

Any public acknowledgment would require your explicit permission and would describe only the exact scope reviewed.
```

## Case 12: They do not respond after agreeing to review

Wait at least 5 business days.

Use:

```text
Hi [Name],

I wanted to follow up briefly in case it would be useful for me to send a smaller review packet for Campus Evidence Lab.

I can send 10 sample records plus the exact questions I’m hoping a reviewer could pressure-test.

Thank you again,
Maximilian
```

## Internal Logging Rule

After any meaningful reply, capture:

- date
- person / organization
- scope offered
- scope completed
- feedback type
- next action
- acknowledgment permission status

If the reply leads to a concrete change, connect that change to:

- the updates page
- the acknowledgments policy
- the tracker

## Success Standard

The best response is not broad praise.

The best response is a narrow, documented critique that leads to a visible improvement and, later, a permission-based acknowledgment.
