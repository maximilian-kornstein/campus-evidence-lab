## Personal Statement / Supplemental Short Description

I built Campus Evidence Lab after experiencing antisemitism and deciding that a serious response should look like disciplined public evidence infrastructure rather than outrage. The project organizes scattered public records into searchable event pages, school dossiers, source pages, reproducible exports, and review workflows. I intentionally designed it not to rank schools or claim prevalence, safety, or legal conclusions. The point was to build something technically real and publicly accountable around a difficult subject.

## Additional Information Section

## Interview Answer

I wanted to build something that imposed discipline on a subject people often talk about loosely. Instead of making an opinion platform or a ranking system, I built an archive that forces records to be tied to public sources, explicit categories, snapshot hashes, and visible limitations. The technically hard part was the data model and workflow, but the intellectually hard part was deciding what the project should refuse to claim. I think that restraint is part of what makes the project serious.

## “Why did you build this?” Answer

I experienced antisemitism and realized that people often discuss campus civil-rights issues with a lot of emotion but weak documentation. I wanted to respond by building something more rigorous: a public-source archive with explicit standards, reproducible artifacts, and strong limits on overclaiming.

## “What makes it unusual?” Answer

Most student projects stop at a website or a dataset. This one includes public-source ingestion, event and school pages, source pages, briefs, exports, source audits, snapshot hashes, correction paths, reviewer workflows, and explicit limits on what the system should not claim.

## “What did you learn?” Answer

I learned that building credible public-interest software is as much about what you refuse to imply as what you choose to show. Good technical systems need boundaries, not just features.

## If Asked What Comes Next

The next step is turning technical execution into institutional credibility: outside review, visible incorporation of feedback, one or two stronger research outputs, and documented third-party use.

## Lines To Avoid

Do not write:

- I built the definitive database of campus discrimination.
- I measured which colleges are safest or most hateful.
- I proved prevalence of campus hate.
- I built a system that tells students where they are safe.
- major organizations endorsed the project.

## Best Default Tone

The strongest tone is:

- technical
- restrained
- evidence-focused
- public-interest oriented
- ambitious without sounding inflated
