# Reviewer Outreach System

This file is the working outreach system for turning cold contact into documented review and third-party use.

The objective is not praise, partnership language, coverage by default, or vague interest. The objective is:

1. get a reply
2. convert the reply into one narrow use case or review
3. capture what they tried, needed, or found unclear
4. incorporate feedback visibly
5. ask for acknowledgment permission only after real use or review exists

## Core Ask

For journalists, student newsrooms, researchers, and organizations that may actually use the archive, lead with the smallest real use ask:

> Would you be willing to try Campus Evidence Lab for one real reporting, research, or issue-tracking question and tell me what would make it more useful?

For reviewers whose main value is methodological scrutiny, keep the smallest critical review ask:

> Would someone be willing to review one page or 10 sample records and tell me what feels weakest, overstated, missing, risky, or methodologically immature?

Do not default to:

- "Would you partner with us?"
- "Would you endorse this?"
- "Would you review the whole project?"
- "Would you cover this?"
- "Would you say something positive?"

## Initial Email: Default Third-Party Use Version

Subject options:

- Could Campus Evidence Lab be useful for your work?
- Trying to make Campus Evidence Lab useful outside my own hands
- Small use-test request: Campus Evidence Lab

Template:

```text
Hi [Name/Team],

I’m reaching out because your work seems close to one of the real use cases for Campus Evidence Lab, a public-source archive of campus civil-rights records.

I’m not asking for endorsement, coverage, or a partnership. I’m trying to learn whether the archive is actually useful to people outside my own workflow.

The smallest useful ask is:

Could you try it for one real question you already care about and tell me what would make it more usable?

Useful feedback could be as simple as:

1. what you searched for
2. what was easy or confusing
3. what would make the archive more useful for reporting, research, routing, or issue tracking

The archive is meant for documentation and source navigation, not rankings, safety scores, or prevalence claims.

Public MVP:
https://campusevidencelab.org/

Research guide:
https://campusevidencelab.org/research-guide/

Press / research brief:
https://campusevidencelab.org/press/

If useful, I can also send a short 5-record sample relevant to your area.

Thank you,
Maximilian Kornstein
maxkornstein04@gmail.com
```

## Initial Email: Default Reviewer Version

Use this when the recipient is unlikely to use the archive directly but can improve the standards, language, or evidentiary caution.

Subject options:

- Request for a narrow review of Campus Evidence Lab
- Methodology review request for a public-source campus archive
- Source-audit review request: Campus Evidence Lab

Template:

```text
Hi [Name/Team],

I’m reaching out because your work seems closely related to one part of Campus Evidence Lab, a public-source archive of campus civil-rights records.

I’m not asking for endorsement. I’m asking for one narrow critical review.

The smallest useful asks are:

1. one-page methodology review
2. 10-record source-audit review
3. one-page responsible-use / no-ranking language review

The most useful feedback would be what feels weakest, overstated, missing, risky, or methodologically immature.

https://campusevidencelab.org/

Reviewer brief:
https://campusevidencelab.org/reviewer-brief/

Methodology:
https://campusevidencelab.org/methodology/

If helpful, I can send a 10-record packet and three narrow review questions.

Thank you,
Maximilian Kornstein
maxkornstein04@gmail.com
```

## Audience Variants

### Research center / professor

Use when the reviewer is strongest on method, categorization, public-interest research, or higher-ed framing.

Swap the opener to:

```text
I’m reaching out because your work on higher education, civil rights, public data, or bias documentation seems directly relevant to the way this archive is scoped.
```

Preferred ask:

```text
Would someone be willing to try the archive against one research question and tell me what fields, filters, or source context would make it more useful? If a use-test is not realistic, a narrow methodology review would also help.
```

### Civil-rights organization

Use when the reviewer is strongest on public-interest risk, use limits, category framing, or responsible interpretation.

Swap the opener to:

```text
I’m reaching out because I want the archive’s use limits, source standards, and category language reviewed by people with stronger civil-rights expertise than mine.
```

Preferred ask:

```text
Would someone be willing to try the archive for one issue-tracking or routing question and tell me where it is useful, misleading, too broad, or too easy to misuse?
```

### Journalist / editor / newsroom

Use when the contact may use the archive for reporting rather than methodology critique.

Swap the opener to:

```text
I’m reaching out because your reporting covers higher education, campus climate, civil rights, or public-records questions.
```

Preferred ask:

```text
I’m not asking for coverage by default. I’m asking whether the archive is useful enough for one reporting workflow: finding source-backed records, checking institutional response material, or identifying follow-up questions.
```

Then point them to:

- press brief
- journalist guide
- one memo

### Student media / campus newsroom

Use when the recipient is a student newspaper, campus editor, or tips desk.

Swap the opener to:

```text
I’m reaching out because your newsroom covers campus life, institutional response, student concerns, and public accountability.
```

Preferred ask:

```text
Would an editor or reporter be willing to try the archive for one campus-related search and tell me what would make it easier to use as a reporting resource?
```

Then point them to:

- journalist guide
- school pages
- source pages
- relevant filtered search if available

### Association / routing organization

Use when the recipient may not use the archive directly but can route it to journalists, researchers, campus staff, civil-rights reviewers, or member organizations.

Swap the opener to:

```text
I’m reaching out because your organization supports people who work on higher education, journalism, civil rights, research, or public-interest institutional questions.
```

Preferred ask:

```text
Would you be willing to route this to one person who might actually try the archive in their work and tell me what would make it more useful?
```

### Clinic / legal reviewer

Use when the reviewer is strongest on evidentiary caution, institutional response language, or public-claim limits.

Swap the opener to:

```text
I’m reaching out because the project needs legal-methodological scrutiny around evidentiary caution, public claims, and what the archive should not imply.
```

Preferred ask:

```text
Would someone be willing to review the no-ranking / no-overclaiming language or a 10-record sample for evidentiary caution?
```

```text

I know trying a new archive may not be realistic. Even one sentence on whether this seems useful for reporting, research, routing, or issue tracking would help me decide what to improve next.

https://campusevidencelab.org/

Thank you,
Maximilian Kornstein
```

### Follow-up 2: no reply after another 7-10 days

```text
Hi [Name/Team],

I wanted to make one final follow-up on Campus Evidence Lab in case the reviewer brief is useful to your work or easier to route internally.

If there is a better person who might use the archive for reporting, research, routing, issue tracking, or a narrow methodology review, I would be grateful for a redirect.

Public MVP:
https://campusevidencelab.org/

Thank you,
Maximilian Kornstein
```

Then stop.

## Conversion Rule

Once someone replies positively, stop sending broad context.

Move immediately to one of these:

- ask what use case they want to try
- send a relevant filtered link or school page
- send a 5-record usage sample for their area
- send 10-record packet
- send one-page methodology request
- send one-page no-ranking / use-limit request
- offer a 15-minute call only if they invite it

The moment of reply is where generic outreach should become a narrow use or review task.

## Do Not Do

- Do not imply partnership before review exists.
- Do not ask for “positive feedback.”
- Do not ask for endorsement.
- Do not ask them to review the full site unless they offer.
- Do not send the exact same opener to every recipient.
- Do not list outreach as impact.
- Do not ask for public acknowledgment before real review is complete.
- Do not frame a journalist or newsroom email as a request for an article unless they invite that path.
- Do not treat routing as success until a routed person replies or uses the archive.

## Success Standard

A successful outreach is not “they liked it.”

A successful outreach is:

- they replied
- they used the archive for one narrow task or reviewed one narrow artifact
- they gave feedback, use-oriented reaction, or a concrete redirect
- you logged it
- you improved the project from it
- you asked permission before any public acknowledgment
