# Where Campus Evidence Lab Can Be Wrong

Campus Evidence Lab is built from public sources. That makes the archive inspectable, but it also creates limits that should be visible before anyone relies on the data.

## Public-source bias

The archive sees what becomes public. Private reports, informal complaints, unreported incidents, sealed records, and lived experience may be absent.

## Discoverability bias

Records are easier to include when sources are findable, stable, indexed, and clearly written. Better search visibility can look like more activity even when it is only better documentation.

## School-size and reporting-practice confounding

Larger schools, schools with more public reporting, and schools covered by more public agencies may generate more visible records. Counts should not be read as incident rates.

## Source-type imbalance

Government datasets, OCR summaries, campus safety notices, and university statements have different blind spots. A dataset dominated by one source type can skew what appears measurable.

## Legal/procedural ambiguity

Complaints, findings, settlements, investigations, and guidance documents mean different things. Records must track source wording and avoid implying legal conclusions that the source does not state.

## Community-label ambiguity

Affected-community labels can be too broad, too narrow, or inferred incorrectly. Labels should be reviewed against source text and narrowed only when public sources support narrowing.

## Import and aggregation limits

Structured imports can preserve large public datasets, but imported rows may have less narrative context than individually reported records. Aggregation keys can also overmerge or undermerge records.

## Missing private context

Public records rarely capture all facts, experiences, institutional deliberations, or later outcomes. The archive should not pretend otherwise.

## Counts are not prevalence

Record counts are documentation counts. They are shaped by source availability, reviewer capacity, reporting practices, public agency activity, and discovery paths.

## What the project does to reduce risk

Campus Evidence Lab uses public source links, neutral summaries, confidence labels, verification labels, record hashes, snapshot hashes, source audits, correction workflows, deterministic review samples, and public codebook definitions. These tools reduce risk; they do not eliminate it.
