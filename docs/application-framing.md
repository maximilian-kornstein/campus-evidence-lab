## Additional Information Version

I built Campus Evidence Lab as public evidence infrastructure for campus civil-rights documentation. The project organizes scattered public records into searchable event pages, school dossiers, source pages, downloadable exports, briefs, source audits, and reproducible snapshot artifacts. I intentionally designed it not to rank schools or claim prevalence, safety, or legal conclusions. The goal is to make public documentation more inspectable, correctable, and useful to researchers, journalists, and reviewers.

## Interview Version

I wanted to build something that imposed discipline on a subject people often talk about loosely. Instead of making an opinion platform or a ranking system, I built an archive that forces records to be tied to public sources, explicit categories, snapshot hashes, and visible limitations. The hard part was not just building pages; it was deciding what the project should refuse to claim.

## Why This Matters

The strongest part of the project is not just the record count. It is the combination of:

- technical execution
- public-interest framing
- methodological restraint
- reproducibility
- outside-review readiness
- visible iteration over time

## Safe Claims

These are strong and defensible:

- I built a public-source archive and research infrastructure.
- I designed a reproducible workflow with hashes, changelog artifacts, and static exports.
- I built school dossiers, source pages, reviewer workflows, and journalist-facing guidance.
- I designed the project to avoid rankings, safety scores, and prevalence claims.
- I am seeking outside methodological review rather than claiming authority.

## Claims To Avoid

Do not say:

- I measured which colleges are safest or most hateful.
- I proved prevalence of campus hate.
- I built the definitive database of campus discrimination.
- I partnered with organizations unless a real documented collaboration exists.
- reviewers endorse the project unless they explicitly authorized that language.

## Strong Personal Angle

Use the personal story briefly and then move back to the work:

I experienced antisemitism and wanted to respond by building something rigorous and useful. That pushed me toward public-source evidence infrastructure rather than reactive commentary.

That is enough. Do not over-center the personal story once the project itself is strong.

## Recommended Application Framing

The best framing is:

1. public-interest research infrastructure  
2. evidence standards before scale  
3. disciplined technical execution  
4. responsible handling of a sensitive topic  
5. outside-review readiness

This is stronger than:

- activism by itself
- raw record-count bragging
- AI/blockchain novelty framing
- generic “I built a website” framing

## If Asked What Makes It Unusual

Most student projects stop at presentation. This one includes data modeling, public-source ingestion, page generation, exports, source audits, snapshot hashes, review workflows, correction paths, and explicit limits on what the system should not claim.

## If Asked What Comes Next

The best next steps are external review, visible incorporation of feedback, one or two serious research outputs, and documented third-party use. That is where the project shifts from technically impressive to institutionally credible.
