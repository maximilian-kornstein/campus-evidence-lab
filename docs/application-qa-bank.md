# Campus Evidence Lab Application Q&A Bank

Use these for interviews, supplemental questions, and live conversations. Keep them short unless the prompt clearly invites more.

## What is Campus Evidence Lab?

Campus Evidence Lab is a public-source archive of campus civil-rights records. I built it to turn scattered records into searchable event pages, school dossiers, source pages, reproducible exports, and audit-ready research infrastructure.

## Why did you start it?

I experienced antisemitism and wanted to respond by building something rigorous rather than reactive. The project became a way to impose evidence standards on a sensitive topic people often discuss loosely.

## What problem does it solve?

Public records in this area are scattered across agency releases, university statements, annual security reports, OCR materials, and journalism. The archive makes those records easier to inspect, cite, audit, and challenge without turning them into rankings or safety scores.

## What was hardest?

The hardest part was deciding what the system should refuse to claim. Building pages and exports is one challenge; building a public-interest system with clear methodological limits is a harder one.

## What did you actually build?

I built the archive, event pages, school dossiers, source pages, exports, briefs, source audits, snapshot hashes, correction paths, reviewer workflows, and journalist/researcher guidance.

## What makes it different from a normal student project?

It is not just a website or a dataset. It is a full public-evidence workflow with reproducibility, reviewability, and explicit limits on misuse.

## What are its limits?

It is not a complete census of incidents, it does not rank schools, and it does not claim prevalence, safety, or legal conclusions. It documents public-source records and makes them easier to inspect.

## What are you most proud of?

I’m most proud that I built something technically ambitious without sacrificing restraint. The project is stronger because it has boundaries.

## How has the project changed you?

It made me more careful. I had to think not just like a builder, but like a reviewer and critic of my own system.

## What do you want to do next with it?

The next step is external review, visible incorporation of feedback, stronger research outputs, and documented third-party use.

## How should a recommender describe it?

As a technically substantial public-interest research system that combines engineering, methodological discipline, and careful handling of a sensitive topic.

## Best 20-second version

I built a public-source campus civil-rights archive with 4,000 records, school dossiers, source audits, exports, and review workflows. The unusual part is that I designed it to be auditable and useful without pretending it can rank schools or measure prevalence.
