\section{Identification from sock-puppet audits}\label{sec:identification}

\Cref{sec:estimands} defined amplification at a fixed demand profile. This section asks when a sock-puppet audit identifies it. The audit is the design that \citet{sandvig2014} call a sock-puppet audit, run as a randomised experiment: accounts whose behaviour the researcher scripts are assigned at random to personas and to policies. The design is related to, but distinct from, the counterfactual bots of \citet{hosseinmardi2024}, which replay real users' histories and then follow a scripted rule, rather than being randomised over personas and policies. We proceed in four steps.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item Under four stated conditions, the corrected difference and ratio of mean exposures identify $\mathrm{AA}(d)$ and $\mathrm{AR}(d)$, with explicit estimators and cluster-robust variances; a blocked design extends this to a drifting policy (\cref{thm:identify}).
  \item Puppets share the system they audit. Under bounded interference, the bias of the naive estimator is at most a constant times the puppets' share of the engagement signal, and a randomised-saturation design reduces it (\cref{prop:interference}).
  \item A drifting policy turns the estimand into a time average over the audit window (\cref{sec:id-drift}).
  \item What no fixed-demand audit identifies is stated in \cref{sec:id-limits}, which connects to \cref{thm:decomp,prop:audit-scope}.
\end{enumerate}
The framework is that of potential outcomes \citep{rubin1974,imbensrubin2015}. Interference is treated with the partial-interference framework of \citet{hudgens2008} and the exposure mappings of \citet{aronow2017}. The variance estimator is the cluster-robust estimator of \citet{liang1986}, and the design effect is that of \citet{kish1965}. Measurement error is introduced here only as far as identification requires; \cref{sec:measurement} treats it in full. Proofs are in \cref{app:proofs-design}.

\subsection{The audit design}\label{sec:id-design}

\paragraph{Puppets, cells and waves}
An audit has puppets $u=1,\dots,U$, personas $d\in\mathcal D_A$ and two policies $\pi\in\{\mathrm{alg},\mathrm{ref}\}$, where $\mathrm{ref}$ is the registered reference policy of \cref{def:amplification}. It runs in waves $w=1,\dots,W$ (\cref{sec:prot-randomisation}). Within wave $w$, puppets are assigned completely at random to the cells $(d,\pi)$, with $n_{wd\pi}\ge1$ puppets in cell $(d,\pi)$. Write $Z_u=(D_u,\Pi_u)$ for the assignment of puppet $u$ and $n_{d\pi}=\sum_wn_{wd\pi}$. Every puppet is trained in the same way for its persona. The policy contrast starts with the measurement phase, so training is identical in distribution across arms.

\paragraph{Sessions and exposure}
In the measurement phase, puppet $u$ runs $S$ sessions. Each session has $T$ steps, and at each step the first $k$ positions of the slate are recorded and classified. The \emph{exposure} $E_{us}$ of session $s$ is the share of these $kT$ recorded items with $L_c=1$. It is \eqref{eq:est-exposure} with the recorded positions counted as items. The \emph{flagged share} $Q_{us}$ is the share with $\widehat L_c=1$. Write $\bar E_u=S^{-1}\sum_sE_{us}$ and $\bar Q_u=S^{-1}\sum_sQ_{us}$. Only $\bar Q_u$ is observed for every puppet. The labels $L_c$ are observed only on a validation sample (\cref{sec:prot-validation}).

\paragraph{Potential outcomes}
Let $\bm z=(z_1,\dots,z_U)$ be an assignment of all puppets, and let $\mathcal R_u(\bm z)$ be the record that puppet $u$ would produce under it: its slates, the items it opened, and their true and classifier labels. Everything about the service that the audit does not control during the window (the versions of its policies, the eligible pool, and the behaviour of real users) is the \emph{environment} of the window. Our statements are conditional on the environment. An audit therefore estimates amplification by the policies that actually ran during the window. It does not estimate amplification by some time-invariant ``algorithm''.

\paragraph{Exposure-weighted error rates}
For cell $(d,\pi)$ define the \emph{exposure-weighted} sensitivity and specificity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:id-sesp}
  \Se_\pi(d)=\frac{\E\sum\ind{L_c=1,\widehat L_c=1}}{\E\sum\ind{L_c=1}},\qquad
  \Sp_\pi(d)=\frac{\E\sum\ind{L_c=0,\widehat L_c=0}}{\E\sum\ind{L_c=0}},
\end{equation}
where each sum runs over the $kTS$ recorded positions of one puppet of the cell, and an item recorded twice counts twice. They are the error rates of the classifier on the content that the cell is actually shown. Let $J_\pi(d)=\Se_\pi(d)+\Sp_\pi(d)-1$ be Youden's index, $\mu_\pi(d)=\E\bar E_u$ the mean exposure of the cell, and $q_\pi(d)=\E\bar Q_u$ its mean flagged share. Counting the recorded positions by true and classifier label gives the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:id-q}
  q_\pi(d)=\Se_\pi(d)\,\mu_\pi(d)+\{1-\Sp_\pi(d)\}\{1-\mu_\pi(d)\}=\{1-\Sp_\pi(d)\}+J_\pi(d)\,\mu_\pi(d),
\end{equation}
which holds without assumptions (\cref{lem:id-count}).

\paragraph{Conditions}
The four conditions of \cref{thm:identify} follow.

\begin{assumption}[No interference between puppets]\label{asm:id-nointerf}
Given the environment, $\mathcal R_u(\bm z)=\mathcal R_u(z_u)$ for every puppet $u$, and the records of distinct puppets are independent.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Stable policies, or a blocked design]\label{asm:id-stable}
Either (a) \emph{stability}: the law of $\mathcal R_u(d,\pi)$, given the environment, is the same for every puppet of the window, whatever its wave; or (b) \emph{blocking}: puppets of the same wave and cell have records with a common law $F_{wd\pi}$, and within each wave the calendar times of the measurement sessions have the same distribution in every cell.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Classifier error does not depend on the arm]\label{asm:id-nondiff}
For each audited persona $d$, $\Se_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)=\Se_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)=\Se(d)$ and $\Sp_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)=\Sp_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)=\Sp(d)$, with $J(d)=\Se(d)+\Sp(d)-1>0$, and $(\Se(d),\Sp(d))$ are identified by the validation sample. When $d$ is fixed we write $\Se$, $\Sp$ and $J$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Scripted demand]\label{asm:id-script}
A puppet's actions are a fixed function of its persona, of what it has been shown, and of randomisation that is independent of the assignment, and this function is the same under both policies. In particular, nobody intervenes in a session by hand, and the choice rule of \cref{sec:prot-personas} is applied identically in both arms. Consequently $\bar E_u$ under $z_u=(d,\pi)$ is a draw of $E_u(\pi;d)$ averaged over sessions, and $\E\bar E_u(d,\pi)=\mu_\pi(d)$ of \eqref{eq:est-mu}.
\end{assumption}

\Cref{asm:id-nointerf} is the stable-unit condition of \citet{rubin1974} for a population that shares a learning system. \Cref{prop:interference} relaxes it. \Cref{asm:id-stable}(a) is what \cref{sec:estimands} presumed when it wrote $\mu_\pi$ as a function of $d$ alone, and (b) is what the waves of the protocol deliver (\cref{sec:id-drift}). \Cref{asm:id-nondiff} says that the classifier's error rates, weighted by what each arm is shown, are the same in both arms. It is \emph{not} implied by error rates that depend only on the content: when error rates differ across content types and the arms show different mixtures of types, \eqref{eq:id-sesp} differs by arm (\cref{thm:misclass}(c)). \Cref{asm:id-script} makes the puppet's demand the persona's script and nothing else. It holds by construction when the script is executed by software that is blind to the arm. It fails if an operator steers sessions, or if the script reads the arm.

A fifth condition is needed to read the estimands as statements about people. It is the second part of \cref{asm:demand}.

\begin{assumption}[Transport from puppets to users]\label{asm:id-transport}
For a real user whose demand profile is $d$, the mean exposure under policy $\pi$ equals $\mu_\pi(d)$, the mean exposure of a puppet of persona $d$.
\end{assumption}

\Cref{thm:identify} does not use \cref{asm:id-transport}. Without it, the theorem still identifies amplification for the scripted personas, which is a property of the service's policies evaluated at those scripts. With it, the same numbers describe users who behave like the scripts. The audit cannot test \cref{asm:id-transport}. It fails if the service recognises automated accounts and treats them differently, if new accounts without a social graph are served differently from established ones, or if signals that puppets lack (device history, location history, purchases) enter the policy. Each failure changes the population to which the estimate applies, not its internal validity. We therefore report the estimates as properties of the audited personas, and treat their transport to real users as a separate claim that needs separate evidence.

\subsection{Identification and estimation}\label{sec:id-theorem}

Let $\widehat q_{d\pi}=n_{d\pi}^{-1}\sum_{u:Z_u=(d,\pi)}\bar Q_u$ be the mean flagged share of cell $(d,\pi)$, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:id-est}
  \widehat\mu_{d\pi}=\frac{\widehat q_{d\pi}+\Sp-1}{J},\qquad
  \widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)=\widehat\mu_{d,\mathrm{alg}}-\widehat\mu_{d,\mathrm{ref}},\qquad
  \widehat{\mathrm{AR}}(d)=\frac{\widehat\mu_{d,\mathrm{alg}}}{\widehat\mu_{d,\mathrm{ref}}}.
\end{equation}
These are the mean \emph{corrected} exposures of \citet{rogangladen1978} and their difference and ratio. Here $(\Se,\Sp)$ are treated as known; \cref{thm:misclass} adds the uncertainty of their estimates. Let $\sigma^2_{d\pi}=\Var(\bar Q_u)$ for a puppet of cell $(d,\pi)$, let $\tau^2_{d\pi}=S^{-1}\sum_s\E\{Q_{us}-q_\pi(d)\}^2$ be the mean squared deviation of a single session from the cell mean, and let $\mathrm{deff}_{d\pi}=S\sigma^2_{d\pi}/\tau^2_{d\pi}$ be the design effect of averaging $S$ correlated sessions. If the sessions of a puppet are exchangeable, with pairwise correlation $\gamma_{d\pi}$, then $\mathrm{deff}_{d\pi}=1+(S-1)\gamma_{d\pi}$ \citep{kish1965}.

\begin{theorem}[Identification by randomised persona--policy audits]\label{thm:identify}
Let puppets be randomised to cells within waves as above, and let \cref{asm:id-nointerf,asm:id-script} hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Identification.} Under \cref{asm:id-stable}(a), $q_\pi(d)=\E\{\bar Q_u\mid Z_u=(d,\pi)\}$ is a functional of the law of the observed data. Under \cref{asm:id-nondiff} in addition,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:id-ident}
    \mu_\pi(d)=\frac{q_\pi(d)+\Sp-1}{J},\qquad
    \mathrm{AA}(d)=\frac{q_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)-q_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)}{J},\qquad
    \mathrm{AR}(d)=\frac{q_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)+\Sp-1}{q_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)+\Sp-1}.
  \end{equation}
  Thus, within a persona, $\mathrm{AA}(d)$ depends on the classifier only through $J(d)$, and $\mathrm{AR}(d)$ only through $\Sp(d)$. Every linear combination of cell means is identified, as $\sum_{d,\pi}c_{d\pi}\mu_\pi(d)=\sum_{d,\pi}c_{d\pi}\{q_\pi(d)+\Sp(d)-1\}/J(d)$. This includes the persona-contrast decomposition of \cref{sec:est-audit}: the supply contrast $\mathrm{AA}(d_0)$, the demand contrast $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)-\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}(d_0)$ and the interaction $\mathrm{AA}(d)-\mathrm{AA}(d_0)$. Contrasts across personas need the error rates of each persona. The exposure-weighted rates generally differ between personas, because personas are shown different mixtures of content (\cref{thm:misclass}(c)). The demand contrast therefore needs both $\Se$ and $\Sp$ of both personas, and the interaction needs $J(d)$ and $J(d_0)$.
  \item \emph{Asymptotic normality.} Under \cref{asm:id-stable}(a) and \cref{asm:id-nondiff}, let $n=\sum_{d,\pi}n_{d\pi}\to\infty$ with $n_{d\pi}/n\to p_{d\pi}>0$, while $S$, $T$ and $k$ stay fixed. If $\mu_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)>0$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)>0$, then
  \begin{align*}
    \sqrt n\{\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)-\mathrm{AA}(d)\}&\Rightarrow\mathrm N\Bigl(0,\;\frac{1}{J^2}\sum_{\pi}\frac{\sigma^2_{d\pi}}{p_{d\pi}}\Bigr),\\
    \sqrt n\{\log\widehat{\mathrm{AR}}(d)-\log\mathrm{AR}(d)\}&\Rightarrow\mathrm N\Bigl(0,\;\frac{1}{J^2}\sum_{\pi}\frac{\sigma^2_{d\pi}}{p_{d\pi}\,\mu_\pi(d)^2}\Bigr),
  \end{align*}
  with the sums over $\pi\in\{\mathrm{alg},\mathrm{ref}\}$. The two cell means are asymptotically independent.
  \item \emph{Cluster-robust variance.} In the regression of the session-level $Q_{us}$ on cell indicators, the cluster-robust variance estimator of \citet{liang1986}, with clusters equal to puppets and multiplied by the common small-sample factor $n_{d\pi}/(n_{d\pi}-1)$, equals $s^2_{d\pi}/n_{d\pi}$ when every puppet has the same number $S$ of sessions, where $s^2_{d\pi}$ is the sample variance of the $\bar Q_u$ in the cell. It is consistent, and replacing $\sigma^2_{d\pi}$ by $s^2_{d\pi}$ in (b) gives intervals with asymptotic coverage $1-\alpha$. The estimator that treats the $n_{d\pi}S$ sessions as independent converges, after multiplication by $n_{d\pi}$, to $\tau^2_{d\pi}/S=\sigma^2_{d\pi}/\mathrm{deff}_{d\pi}$. It is inconsistent unless $\mathrm{deff}_{d\pi}=1$, and anticonservative when sessions are positively correlated.
  \item \emph{Blocked design.} Under \cref{asm:id-stable}(b) instead of (a), let $\mu_{w\pi}(d)$ and $q_{w\pi}(d)$ be the means under $F_{wd\pi}$, and let $\omega_w$ be fixed design weights with $\sum_w\omega_w=1$, for example $\omega_w=\sum_\pi n_{wd\pi}/\sum_\pi n_{d\pi}$. Let $\bar\mu_\pi(d)=\sum_w\omega_w\mu_{w\pi}(d)$ be the \emph{window-averaged} mean exposure, and $\overline{\mathrm{AA}}(d)=\bar\mu_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)-\bar\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)$ and $\overline{\mathrm{AR}}(d)=\bar\mu_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)/\bar\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)$ the window-averaged estimands. Assume \cref{asm:id-nondiff} with error rates that may differ by wave but not by arm within a wave. Then (a) holds wave by wave, the blocked estimator $\widehat{\bar\mu}_{d\pi}=\sum_w\omega_w\widehat\mu_{wd\pi}$ is unbiased for $\bar\mu_\pi(d)$, and with $W$ fixed and $n_{wd\pi}\to\infty$ in fixed proportions, (b) holds for $\overline{\mathrm{AA}}(d)$ and $\overline{\mathrm{AR}}(d)$ with $J^{-2}\sigma^2_{d\pi}/p_{d\pi}$ replaced by $\sum_w\omega_w^2\sigma^2_{wd\pi}/(J_w^2p_{wd\pi})$, where $J_w$ is the Youden index of wave $w$, and with $\mu_\pi(d)$ replaced by $\bar\mu_\pi(d)$. The stratified variance estimator $\sum_w\omega_w^2s^2_{wd\pi}/(J_w^2n_{wd\pi})$ is consistent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Randomisation makes $Z_u$ independent of the potential records. Together with \cref{asm:id-nointerf}, it makes the observed $\bar Q_u$ of a puppet in cell $(d,\pi)$ a draw from the law of $\bar Q_u(d,\pi)$. \Cref{asm:id-stable}(a) makes these draws identically distributed across waves, and \cref{asm:id-script} identifies their true-exposure mean with $\mu_\pi(d)$. Identity \eqref{eq:id-q} under \cref{asm:id-nondiff}, solved for $\mu_\pi(d)$, gives \eqref{eq:id-ident}. Parts (b) and (c) are the central limit theorem for independent puppet means, the delta method, and the observation that the cluster-robust sandwich collapses to the between-puppet variance when every cluster has the same number of sessions. Part (d) applies (a)--(c) within waves, which are independent strata. The full proof is in \cref{app:pf-identify}.
\end{proof}

Three remarks interpret the theorem.

\emph{The puppet is the unit.} The sessions of one puppet share its trained profile, so they are positively correlated in most designs. An analysis that treats sessions as independent then understates the variance by the factor $\mathrm{deff}_{d\pi}$, which is $3.7$ for $S=10$ sessions with $\gamma=0.3$. Its intervals are too short by the factor $\mathrm{deff}^{1/2}\approx1.9$, and a nominal $95\%$ interval then covers with probability $2\Phi(1.96/3.7^{1/2})-1\approx0.69$. The design effect need not exceed one: when a puppet's profile drifts away from its training between sessions, the correlation can be negative, and session-level intervals then over-cover. The cluster-robust estimator is correct in both cases. \Cref{sec:simulation} evaluates both estimators on the synthetic recommender.

\emph{The classifier enters differently on the two scales.} By \eqref{eq:id-ident}, a naive difference of flagged shares estimates $J(d)\,\mathrm{AA}(d)$, so it has the sign of $\mathrm{AA}(d)$ whenever $J(d)>0$. A test of $\mathrm{AA}(d)=0$ therefore needs no validation data under \cref{asm:id-nondiff}. The ratio is different. The naive ratio is biased towards one, and the correction needs $\Sp(d)$ and nothing else. Both simplifications hold within a persona only. Across personas they fail, as part (a) states. Puppets of different personas are compared on different content, so a persona contrast corrected with common error rates can be wrong even in sign.

\emph{Unequal sessions.} If puppets complete different numbers of sessions, for example because some are restricted by the service, the session-weighted cell mean and its sandwich no longer coincide with the analysis of puppet means. We analyse puppet means, which weight puppets equally. This remains unbiased if attrition is unrelated to exposure, and attrition is compared across arms as registered (\cref{sec:prot-interference}).

\emph{Persona contrasts are identified, user effects are not.} Part (a) identifies the persona-contrast decomposition of \cref{sec:est-audit} for any two audited personas. It is the decomposition that the protocol registers (\cref{sec:prot-estimands}). Its demand contrast compares two scripts that the researcher chose. \Cref{sec:id-limits} returns to what this does not identify.

\subsection{Interference through the shared recommender}\label{sec:id-interference}

Puppets share the system they audit. When a puppet opens an item, its engagement can enter the item's global popularity, the item-to-item associations, or the next model update, and through these it can change what other puppets are shown. \Cref{asm:id-nointerf} then fails. We model the failure with an exposure mapping \citep{aronow2017} and bound its effect. \citet{savje2021} show that standard estimators remain consistent for an expected average effect under limited interference of unknown form. Here we need more: a bound on the distance between that effect and the effect without the audit.

\paragraph{The load}
Let the protocol separate waves by gaps longer than the memory of the learning system, so that puppets of different waves do not interfere. Waves are then the groups of partial interference \citep{hudgens2008}. Within wave $w$, let $\ell_w\in[0,1)$ be the \emph{load}, the share of the engagement signal from which the service learns, in the region of the content space that the wave's personas visit, that is generated by the wave's puppets. A service with millions of users can still have a substantial load in a niche: what matters is the puppets' share of the engagement with the items they open, not their share of all traffic. Under the avoid-on-flag rule (\cref{sec:prot-personas}), puppets add engagement only to items that the classifier does not flag.

\begin{assumption}[Bounded interference]\label{asm:id-bounded}
Waves do not interfere with one another. Within a wave, given its load $\ell_w$, the records of distinct puppets are independent, and the record of a puppet with assignment $(d,\pi)$ depends on the other puppets' assignments only through $\ell_w$, with mean exposure $m_{d\pi}(\ell_w)$ and classifier error rates that do not depend on the arm. Moreover, there are constants $\xi_\pi\ge0$ and $\xi_\Delta\ge0$ such that for all $\ell\in[0,\ell_{\max}]$,
\[
  |m_{d\pi}(\ell)-m_{d\pi}(0)|\le\xi_\pi\,\ell,\qquad
  \bigl|\{m_{d,\mathrm{alg}}(\ell)-m_{d,\mathrm{ref}}(\ell)\}-\{m_{d,\mathrm{alg}}(0)-m_{d,\mathrm{ref}}(0)\}\bigr|\le\xi_\Delta\,\ell .
\]
\end{assumption}

The no-audit quantities are $\mu_\pi(d)=m_{d\pi}(0)$: the mean exposure that a single puppet, too small to move the system, would see. Their contrasts $\mathrm{AA}^0(d)$ and $\mathrm{AR}^0(d)$ are the estimands of \cref{def:amplification}. The constant $\xi_\pi$ is the largest change in mean exposure under policy $\pi$ per unit of load. Taking $\xi_\pi$ and $\xi_\Delta$ to be the smallest constants for which these inequalities hold, $\xi_\Delta\le\xi_{\mathrm{alg}}+\xi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and $\xi_\Delta$ can be much smaller when load moves both arms in the same direction. A reference that does not learn from engagement, such as a reverse-chronological feed of followed accounts or a random draw from the pool, has $\xi_{\mathrm{ref}}=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Interference bias, design remedies and sensitivity]\label{prop:interference}
Let \cref{asm:id-stable}(b), \cref{asm:id-nondiff,asm:id-script,asm:id-bounded} hold, with waves randomised as in \cref{thm:identify}(d), wave weights $\omega_w$ and loads $\ell_w\le\ell_{\max}$ fixed by the design of the waves. Let $\bar\ell=\sum_w\omega_w\ell_w$ be the average load, and $M_\pi(d)=\sum_w\omega_wm_{d\pi}(\ell_w)$. All statements are for the given waves; the corrected cell means are linear in the data, so they are exact statements about expectations.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Bias bound.} The blocked estimator $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)$ of \cref{thm:identify}(d) has expectation $M_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)-M_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)$, and
  \[
    \bigl|\E\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)-\mathrm{AA}^0(d)\bigr|\le\xi_\Delta\,\bar\ell\le(\xi_{\mathrm{alg}}+\xi_{\mathrm{ref}})\,\bar\ell .
  \]
  If $M_\pi(d)>\xi_\pi\bar\ell$ for both $\pi$, the ratio of expected corrected means satisfies $|\log\{M_{\mathrm{alg}}(d)/M_{\mathrm{ref}}(d)\}-\log\mathrm{AR}^0(d)|\le\sum_\pi\xi_\pi\bar\ell/\{M_\pi(d)-\xi_\pi\bar\ell\}$. Each inequality for $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}$ is attained by some $m$ satisfying \cref{asm:id-bounded}.
  \item \emph{Direction at a fixed state.} Suppose a policy fills each recorded position independently, choosing item $c$ with probability proportional to $f_c\,p_c^{\,\beta}$, where $f_c\ge0$ is fixed by the puppet's current state, $p_c$ is the item's popularity and $\beta>0$. Suppose the wave's puppets shift popularity to $p=(1-\ell)p^0+\ell p^{\mathrm P}$, where $p^{\mathrm P}$ is the popularity generated by the puppets' openings. Let $\chi(\ell)$ be the probability that a position shows an item with $L_c=1$. (i) If $p^{\mathrm P}$ puts no mass on items with $L_c=1$, then $\chi(\ell)$ is non-increasing in $\ell$. (ii) If $\beta=1$, then for every $p^{\mathrm P}$, $\chi(\ell)=w_\ell\,\chi(0)+(1-w_\ell)\,\chi^{\mathrm P}$, where $\chi^{\mathrm P}=\sum_{c:L_c=1}f_cp^{\mathrm P}_c/\sum_cf_cp^{\mathrm P}_c$ and $w_\ell\in(0,1]$ decreases from $w_0=1$. Exposure then rises with load if and only if $\chi^{\mathrm P}>\chi(0)$, which a small share of puppet engagement on false negatives with large $f_c$ can produce.
  \item \emph{Randomised saturation.} Suppose waves are randomised to two loads $\ell_1<\ell_2$, with weights $\omega^{(1)}$ and $\omega^{(2)}$ summing to one, and let $\mathrm{AA}(\ell)=m_{d,\mathrm{alg}}(\ell)-m_{d,\mathrm{ref}}(\ell)$ be twice differentiable on $[0,\ell_2]$ with $|\mathrm{AA}''|\le\zeta$. The extrapolated estimator $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{ex}}=\{\ell_2\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(\ell_1)-\ell_1\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(\ell_2)\}/(\ell_2-\ell_1)$, built from the blocked estimators at each load, satisfies
  \[
    \bigl|\E\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\mathrm{AA}^0(d)\bigr|\le\tfrac12\,\zeta\,\ell_1\ell_2,
  \]
  and its variance is $\{\ell_2^2V_1+\ell_1^2V_2\}/(\ell_2-\ell_1)^2$, where $V_j$ is the variance of $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(\ell_j)$. The slope $\{\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(\ell_2)-\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(\ell_1)\}/(\ell_2-\ell_1)$ estimates the average derivative of $\mathrm{AA}$ on $[\ell_1,\ell_2]$, and a test that it is zero is a test of differential interference.
  \item \emph{Sensitivity analysis.} For a postulated $\xi_\Delta$, the interval $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)\pm\{\xi_\Delta\bar\ell+z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat{\mathrm{se}}\}$ covers $\mathrm{AA}^0(d)$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$, to the accuracy of the normal approximation to $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)$ given in \cref{thm:identify}. The sign of $\mathrm{AA}^0(d)$ is established at level $\alpha$ for every $\xi_\Delta<\xi^\ast=(|\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)|-z_{1-\alpha/2}\widehat{\mathrm{se}})_+/\bar\ell$, the \emph{breakdown value} of the conclusion.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) The blocked estimator is unbiased for the wave-averaged means at the given loads, and the triangle inequality with \cref{asm:id-bounded} bounds their distance from the no-audit means. The ratio bound is the mean value theorem for the logarithm. (b) In (i), the weight of every item with $L_c=1$ is multiplied by $(1-\ell)^\beta$, and the weight of every other item by at least $(1-\ell)^\beta$. In (ii), numerator and denominator are linear in $\ell$. (c) Linear extrapolation to $\ell=0$ from two nodes has the Lagrange remainder $\frac12\mathrm{AA}''(\eta)\ell_1\ell_2$. (d) The interval is the union of Wald intervals over the identified set of (a). Details are in \cref{app:pf-interference}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Design remedies}
\Cref{prop:interference} turns the interference problem into a problem of keeping $\bar\ell$ small and measuring what remains.
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
  \item \emph{Minimal engagement.} Puppets that record slates, open one item per step with fixed dwell time, and never like, follow or share contribute the least engagement signal per session. They minimise $\ell_w$ for a given number of sessions. This is also the footprint rule of \cref{sec:eth-footprint}.
  \item \emph{Waves as clusters in time.} Separating waves by a washout longer than the system's memory makes waves independent groups. Staggering puppets within a wave lowers the instantaneous load. Waves can also be assigned to policies as whole clusters, which is a switchback design \citep{bojinov2023}. Each arm is then observed in a wave in which every puppet follows it, so interference between arms is replaced by interference within arms. The price is that arms are compared across waves, so drift enters the contrast unless waves are numerous and randomised.
  \item \emph{Randomised saturation.} Randomising the number of puppets per wave between two levels \citep{baird2018} identifies the slope in part (c). It allows a test of interference and a bias-reduced estimate. With $\ell_2=2\ell_1$ and the waves split equally between the two loads, the extrapolated estimator has about ten times the variance of the blocked estimator that uses all waves at one load, so it is a robustness analysis rather than the primary estimator.
  \item \emph{Sentinels.} Untrained puppets run concurrently with the trained ones (\cref{sec:prot-interference}). The slope of their exposure in the load estimates the interference for the sentinel profile. It is evidence about $\xi_\pi$, not a bound on it. Sentinels are not the audited personas, and their sensitivity to load can be smaller.
\end{enumerate}

Part (b) shows why the direction of the bias cannot be taken for granted. If puppets never engaged with antisemitic items, the popularity they add would go only to other items, and at any fixed state a popularity-weighted policy would show less antisemitic content as the load rose. But the avoid-on-flag rule opens false negatives (\cref{sec:prot-personas}), so some puppet engagement does reach antisemitic items. By (ii), what matters is whether that engagement is more concentrated on antisemitic items, weighted by the policy's relevance factors, than the baseline exposure itself. When exposure is rare, a small share of engagement on a few relevant false negatives can exceed it and raise exposure. Part (b) also holds only at a fixed state. Over a session, the puppet's state responds to what it is shown, and the direction need not persist. We therefore make no general claim about the sign of the interference bias. When both policies use popularity, the shifts partly cancel in $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}(d)$, which is why $\xi_\Delta$ can be much smaller than $\xi_{\mathrm{alg}}+\xi_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Keeping false negatives out of the puppets' openings, through a conservative threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{safe}}$, makes (i) the relevant case. \Cref{sec:simulation} measures the interference bias, and the effect of the remedies, on the synthetic recommender, whose engagement-driven updates make the load explicit.

\paragraph{Reporting}
The protocol reports $\bar\ell$, or an upper bound on it computed from public engagement counts of the opened items. It reports the sentinel slope and, where a saturation design is used, the slope of part (c). It also reports the breakdown value $\xi^\ast$ of part (d), with a statement of the values of $\xi_\Delta$ that the sentinel and saturation evidence makes plausible. Because exposure to antisemitic content is rare, the ratio bound in part (a) is informative only if $\xi_\pi\bar\ell$ is small relative to $M_\pi(d)$. Interference that shifts exposure by one percentage point is negligible for a feed in which antisemitic items have a share of one third, and decisive for a feed in which they have a share of one per cent.

\subsection{Drift: what you audit is a moving target}\label{sec:id-drift}

Services change their policies continually, by retraining, by experiments on subsets of users, and by announced changes. Under drift, \cref{asm:id-stable}(a) fails, and the question is what an audit spread over weeks estimates.

\paragraph{The blocked design}
Under \cref{asm:id-stable}(b), \cref{thm:identify}(d) says that the blocked estimator targets $\overline{\mathrm{AA}}(d)=\sum_w\omega_w\mathrm{AA}_w(d)$. This is the additive amplification averaged over the waves of the window, and within each wave over the calendar times at which its sessions ran. The same holds for the ratio of window-averaged means, $\overline{\mathrm{AR}}(d)$. This is not the average of the wave ratios, for the reason given after \cref{lem:est-agg}. Blocking does not remove drift from the estimand. It ensures that drift affects both arms equally, so that it cannot be mistaken for amplification, and it removes between-wave variation from the variance. An unblocked design, in which arms are run at different times, confounds amplification with drift. A completely randomised design without blocks remains unbiased for the time average, with a larger variance.

\paragraph{Diagnosing drift}
The wave-level estimates $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}_w(d)$ are independent across waves under \cref{asm:id-stable}(b). The statistic $Q_{\mathrm{drift}}=\sum_w(\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}_w-\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{iv}})^2/\widehat V_w$, with $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{iv}}$ the inverse-variance-weighted mean, is asymptotically $\chi^2_{W-1}$ when $\mathrm{AA}_w$ does not vary. It is a test of drift in amplification, as distinct from drift in exposure that is common to both arms. Sentinels track the latter. Changes that the service announces are logged (\cref{sec:prot-randomisation}). When such a change falls inside the window, the waves before and after it are reported separately as well as together.

\paragraph{Rotation when cells cannot share a wave}
If capacity does not allow every cell in every wave, cells can be rotated across waves in a balanced incomplete block design, in which every pair of cells shares a wave equally often. Within-wave contrasts then identify every pairwise contrast, and the window average is estimated by combining them. The contrast between $\mathrm{alg}$ and $\mathrm{ref}$ for the same persona should always share a wave, because it is the contrast that drift would otherwise confound.

\paragraph{The moving target}
An audit identifies amplification by the policies that ran during its window. The estimand is indexed by the window, and so is every conclusion. Nothing in the design licenses extrapolation to the policies that run after it. The Markov model of \cref{sec:markov} extrapolates across session lengths and starting states, not across policy versions. Sequential monitoring (\cref{alg:sequential}) shortens the window and therefore narrows the target, which is a further argument for it. Under drift, its guarantee holds for the window-averaged estimand only approximately; we return to this in \cref{sec:meas-sequential}.

\subsection{What audits cannot identify}\label{sec:id-limits}

\Cref{thm:identify} identifies $\mu_\pi(d)$ for the audited personas $d\in\mathcal D_A$ and both policies, and hence every contrast among them. By \cref{prop:audit-scope}, this identifies amplification for any declared mixture $Q$ of audited personas, and the supply effect $\tau_{\mathrm{sup}}$ of \cref{thm:decomp} if the distribution of demand formed under the reference were known and concentrated on $\mathcal D_A$. It does not identify the demand-shaping effect $\tau_{\mathrm{dem}}$, the feedback interaction $\iota$, or the total effect of the algorithm on real users, because a puppet's demand does not respond to what it is shown. The persona-contrast decomposition identified by \cref{thm:identify}(a) has the same algebra as \cref{thm:decomp}, but its ``demand'' is a difference between two scripts chosen by the researcher, not a change in demand caused by the algorithm. Statements about real users require \cref{asm:id-transport} in addition, and statements about the algorithm's total effect on them require either the designs listed after \cref{prop:audit-scope} or the sensitivity analysis of \cref{prop:audit-scope}(c).
