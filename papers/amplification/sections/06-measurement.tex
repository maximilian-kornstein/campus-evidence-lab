\section{Measurement: misclassification and sequential inference}\label{sec:measurement}

An audit sees exposure through a classifier. This section corrects for the classifier's errors (\cref{sec:meas-misclass}), turns the corrected estimators into an audit that can stop as soon as the evidence is decisive (\cref{sec:meas-sequential}), and sizes the audit (\cref{sec:meas-power}). \Cref{thm:misclass} gives the bias of naive estimators and shows that error rates that differ by arm can push the naive ratio in either direction. \Cref{alg:sequential} and \cref{thm:sequential} give a confidence sequence for the amplification ratio that remains approximately valid when the audit stops early, and \cref{prop:power} gives the numbers of puppets, sessions and codes in closed form. Throughout, the persona $d$ is fixed and suppressed, the arms are $\pi\in\{a,r\}$ with $a=\mathrm{alg}$ and $r=\mathrm{ref}$, and the notation is that of \cref{sec:id-design}. In particular $q_\pi=(1-\Sp_\pi)+J_\pi\mu_\pi$ by \eqref{eq:id-q}.

\subsection{Misclassification}\label{sec:meas-misclass}

The correction of a prevalence for a test with known sensitivity and specificity is due to \citet{rogangladen1978}. Double sampling, in which a second-phase subsample of the first-phase units is measured more accurately, is due to \citet{neyman1938}, and \citet{tenenbein1970} applied it to binomial data subject to misclassification. The double-sampling estimator below is also the simplest case of recent methods that combine many machine labels with a few gold-standard labels, prediction-powered inference \citep{angelopoulos2023} and design-based supervised learning \citep{egami2023}. Our contribution is to work out what these imply for a \emph{ratio} of two prevalences, measured on two arms that show different content.

\paragraph{Validation designs}
In an \emph{external} design, the error rates are estimated from coded items that are independent of the audit's measurement data: $\widehat\Se$ is the share flagged among $n_1$ coded items with $L_c=1$, and $\widehat\Sp$ the share unflagged among $n_0$ coded items with $L_c=0$. In an \emph{internal} design, which the protocol uses, coded items are drawn from each arm's own recorded exposures, stratified by classifier output. Either way, the target is the exposure-weighted error rate \eqref{eq:id-sesp} of each arm, so a coded item must represent exposures, not distinct items: an item that appeared in many recorded positions is weighted by its number of appearances divided by its inclusion probability.

\begin{theorem}[Misclassification: attenuation, correction and differential error]\label{thm:misclass}
Let $\mu_r>0$ and $J_\pi>0$ for both arms.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Naive estimands under non-differential error.} Under \cref{asm:id-nondiff}, the naive additive contrast is $q_a-q_r=J\,\mathrm{AA}$, and the naive ratio is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:mc-attenuation}
    \mathrm{AR}^{\mathrm{nv}}=\frac{q_a}{q_r}=w_0+(1-w_0)\,\mathrm{AR},\qquad w_0=\frac{1-\Sp}{q_r}\in[0,1).
  \end{equation}
  The naive ratio is a convex combination of $1$ and $\mathrm{AR}$, so it is attenuated towards one. The weight $w_0$ depends on the classifier only through $\Sp$ and $q_r$, and $w_0=0$ if and only if $\Sp=1$.
  \item \emph{Corrected estimators.} Under \cref{asm:id-nondiff}, with $(\widehat\Se,\widehat\Sp)$ from an external design, the corrected $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}=\widehat\mu_a-\widehat\mu_r$ and $\log\widehat{\mathrm{AR}}$, with $\widehat\mu_\pi=(\widehat q_\pi+\widehat\Sp-1)/\widehat J$, are consistent and asymptotically normal. Their delta-method variances are given in \cref{prop:mc-variance} (\cref{app:pf-misclass}); the estimation error of $\widehat\Se$ does not enter the variance of $\log\widehat{\mathrm{AR}}$.
  \item \emph{Differential error.} Without \cref{asm:id-nondiff}, let $r_\pi=q_\pi/\mu_\pi=\Se_\pi+(1-\Sp_\pi)(1-\mu_\pi)/\mu_\pi$. Then, exactly,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:mc-diff}
    \mathrm{AR}^{\mathrm{nv}}=\mathrm{AR}\cdot\frac{r_a}{r_r},\qquad
    \mathrm{AR}^{\mathrm{pool}}=\frac{J_a\mu_a+\overline{\Sp}-\Sp_a}{J_r\mu_r+\overline{\Sp}-\Sp_r},
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathrm{AR}^{\mathrm{pool}}$ is the limit of the corrected ratio computed with error rates $(\overline{\Se},\overline{\Sp})$ pooled over the two arms. Either can lie on the other side of one from $\mathrm{AR}$, and \cref{lem:mc-range} gives the range of $\mathrm{AR}^{\mathrm{nv}}$ compatible with bounds on the error rates. If content falls into types $k$ with labels $L_k$ and type-level flag rates $q_k$ common to both arms, and arm $\pi$ shows type $k$ with exposure share $e_{\pi k}$, then $\Se_\pi=\mu_\pi^{-1}\sum_{k:L_k=1}e_{\pi k}q_k$ and $1-\Sp_\pi=(1-\mu_\pi)^{-1}\sum_{k:L_k=0}e_{\pi k}q_k$. \Cref{asm:id-nondiff} then fails unless, within each label, the two arms' mixtures of types have the same average flag rate, which fails generically when the arms mix types with different $q_k$ in different proportions.
  \item \emph{Stratified validation.} Let the error rates be estimated separately in each arm, by an external design per arm or by an internal design, with estimators consistent for $(\Se_\pi,\Sp_\pi)$. Then $\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{st}}_\pi=(\widehat q_\pi+\widehat\Sp_\pi-1)/\widehat J_\pi$ is consistent for $\mu_\pi$, whether or not \cref{asm:id-nondiff} holds, and so are the resulting $\widehat{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{st}}$ and $\widehat{\mathrm{AR}}^{\mathrm{st}}$. In the internal design that codes $m_{\pi1}$ flagged and $m_{\pi0}$ unflagged exposures of arm $\pi$, and weights them by the arm's recorded flagged and unflagged counts, the corrected estimator equals the double-sampling estimator exactly,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:mc-ds}
    \frac{\widehat q_\pi+\widehat\Sp_\pi-1}{\widehat\Se_\pi+\widehat\Sp_\pi-1}=\widehat q_\pi\,\widehat\PPV_\pi+(1-\widehat q_\pi)\,\widehat\FOR_\pi,
  \end{equation}
  where $\widehat\PPV_\pi$ and $\widehat\FOR_\pi$ are the shares with $L_c=1$ among the coded flagged and coded unflagged exposures. If the coding is done within each puppet, the puppet-level version of the right side, the \emph{double-sampled exposure} $\widetilde E_u$, is conditionally unbiased for the puppet's true exposure $\bar E_u$.
  \item \emph{Confirmed-positive form.} Let $\PPV_\pi$ be the exposure-weighted share of true positives among flagged exposures of arm $\pi$. Then $q_\pi\PPV_\pi=\Se_\pi\mu_\pi$ exactly, and hence
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:mc-confirmed}
    \mathrm{AR}=\frac{1}{\lambda_S}\cdot\frac{q_a\PPV_a}{q_r\PPV_r},\qquad \lambda_S=\frac{\Se_a}{\Se_r}.
  \end{equation}
  If the sensitivities are equal, $\mathrm{AR}$ is identified by the flagged shares and coded flagged exposures alone, whatever the specificities of the two arms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) Substitute \eqref{eq:id-q} into $q_a/q_r$. (b) The delta method; in the ratio, $J$ cancels. (c) Divide $q_\pi$ by $\mu_\pi$; $r_\pi$ is increasing in $\Se_\pi$ and decreasing in $\Sp_\pi$, and the four rates vary independently over a rectangle. (d) Consistency is the continuous mapping theorem applied arm by arm, and \eqref{eq:mc-ds} is algebra. (e) Both sides of $q_\pi\PPV_\pi=\Se_\pi\mu_\pi$ equal the expected share of recorded positions that are flagged and truly antisemitic. The full proof is in \cref{app:pf-misclass}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Rare content makes specificity decisive}
When antisemitic content is rare, even a highly specific classifier makes $w_0$ large. With $\mu_r=0.02$, $\Se=0.85$ and $\Sp=0.98$, $w_0=0.55$: a true ratio of $1.5$ appears as $1.23$, while the naive difference is attenuated only by $J=0.83$. In the variance of $\log\widehat{\mathrm{AR}}$ \eqref{eq:mc-varAR}, the specificity term is multiplied by $(1/\mu_a-1/\mu_r)^2$: with $\mathrm{AR}=1.5$ and $n_0=1{,}500$ coded negatives it alone contributes $0.073$ to the standard deviation. The delta method is also less accurate than usual, being good only if $\mathrm{cv}_{\Sp}=v_{\Sp}^{1/2}/(J\mu_r)$, the coefficient of variation that estimating $\Sp$ gives the corrected reference exposure, is small; here it is $0.22$. We recommend reporting $\mathrm{cv}_{\Sp}$ and a bootstrap alongside the delta-method interval (\cref{sec:simulation} reports the understatement).

\paragraph{Differential error is the rule, not the exception}
Part (c) formalises the problem raised in \cref{sec:bg-classifier}: if the algorithm shifts the mix of content types, arm-level error rates differ, even though no type-level error rate does. \Cref{tab:mc-signflip} gives two invented examples. In scenario~I, the algorithm shows more coded antisemitic content, which the classifier misses: the true ratio is $1.8$, but the naive ratio is $0.97$ and the corrected ratio with pooled error rates is $0.94$. In scenario~II, the algorithm shows less antisemitic content and much more counter-speech: the true ratio is $0.57$, but the naive ratio is $1.24$ and the pooled correction makes things worse, at $1.69$. Correcting with the wrong error rates is not a safe default. By part (d), validation stratified by arm recovers the true ratio in both cases.

\paragraph{Validation by arm, and where to spend it}
Part (d) is why the protocol stratifies validation by arm. The internal design needs no error rates that transfer from elsewhere, and done within puppets it yields corrected puppet-level exposures whose cluster-robust variance contains the validation uncertainty. Its cost lies in the unflagged stratum, where false negatives are rare, so score bands with the allocation of \citet{neyman1934} concentrate the coding where they are. Part (e) exploits the asymmetry between the strata: the ratio needs the flagged stratum of each arm and one number about the unflagged strata, $\lambda_S$. Differential specificity is then corrected automatically (scenario~II: $\lambda_S=1.02$, and \eqref{eq:mc-confirmed} with $\lambda_S=1$ gives $0.59$ against $0.57$), while differential sensitivity enters as a single factor (scenario~I: $\lambda_S=0.62$, and setting it to one gives $1.12$).

\subsection{Sequential, anytime-valid audits}\label{sec:meas-sequential}

An audit that stops as soon as its evidence is decisive uses fewer accounts and sessions, which matters for its footprint (\cref{sec:eth-footprint}) and for the moving target of \cref{sec:id-drift}. Stopping on the data invalidates a fixed-sample interval, however, because a monitored fixed-sample analysis performs repeated significance tests. Sequential tests go back to \citet{wald1945}. A \emph{confidence sequence} \citep{darling1967} is a sequence of sets $C_n$, indexed here by the number $n$ of puppet pairs, with $\Pr(\theta\in C_n\text{ for all }n\ge1)\ge1-\alpha$. It remains valid at any stopping time. Modern constructions use nonnegative supermartingales and Ville's inequality \citep{ville1939}, through normal mixtures \citep{robbins1970}, nonparametric boundaries \citep{howard2021} or betting \citep{waudbysmith2024betting}. \citet{waudbysmith2024asymp} give the asymptotic analogue of the central-limit interval, and \citet{ramdas2023} review the field.

\paragraph{The puppet outcome}
The sequence is built from one number per puppet, an \emph{outcome} $O_u\in[0,1]$ computed from the puppet's own record and codes, whose mean in arm $\pi$ is proportional to $\mu_\pi$. Let $\vartheta=\E O_a/\E O_r=\lambda\,\mathrm{AR}$, where the factor $\lambda$ is declared to lie in a registered range $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$. Two outcomes qualify.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item The \emph{double-sampled exposure} $O_u=\widetilde E_u$ of \cref{thm:misclass}(d), computed from $m$ coded flagged and $m'$ coded unflagged positions of the puppet. It is conditionally unbiased for $\bar E_u$, so $\lambda=1$ and no assumption about error rates is needed. It is the protocol's primary outcome (\cref{sec:prot-summary}).
  \item The \emph{confirmed-flagged exposure} $O_u=Y_u=\bar Q_u\widehat P_u$, where $\widehat P_u$ is the share confirmed as antisemitic among $m$ flagged positions of the puppet drawn uniformly without replacement (all of them if it has fewer). Given the record, $Y_u$ is unbiased for the share of the puppet's recorded positions that are flagged and truly antisemitic, so $\E Y_u=\Se_\pi\mu_\pi$ (\cref{lem:seq-unbiased}) and $\lambda=\lambda_S$ by \cref{thm:misclass}(e). It needs no unflagged codes, but its range for $\lambda_S$ is an assumption.
\end{enumerate}
Pairs $i$ consist of one puppet per arm from the same wave and persona, with outcomes $(O_{a,i},O_{r,i})$. For $s\ge0$ and a fixed tuning constant $a>0$, let
\[
  h(s)=\bigl\{2(1+as)\,a^{-1}\log\bigl((1+as)^{1/2}/\alpha\bigr)\bigr\}^{1/2},
\]
the normal-mixture boundary of \citet{robbins1970} in units of the planned horizon.

\begin{algorithm}[tbp]
\caption{Anytime-valid audit of $\mathrm{AR}(d)$ for one persona, from a puppet outcome $O_u$}\label{alg:sequential}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require level $\alpha$; margin $\delta>1$; wave budget $W_{\max}$; burn-in waves $b\ge1$; planned pairs $n^\ast$ and tuning constant $a$; outcome $O_u\in[0,1]$ with range $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$ for $\lambda$
\State $\mathrm{LB}\gets-\infty$; $\mathrm{UB}\gets+\infty$
\For{$w=1,2,\dots,W_{\max}$}
  \State run wave $w$; code each puppet of persona $d$ as its outcome requires, and compute $O_u$
  \State pair each $\mathrm{alg}$ puppet with a distinct $\mathrm{ref}$ puppet of the wave, at random; append $(O_{a,i},O_{r,i})$; $n\gets$ number of pairs
  \State $\bar O_a\gets n^{-1}\sum_{i\le n}O_{a,i}$;\quad $\bar O_r\gets n^{-1}\sum_{i\le n}O_{r,i}$;\quad $\widehat\vartheta_n\gets\bar O_a/\bar O_r$
  \State $\widehat\sigma_n^{\,2}\gets n^{-1}\sum_{i\le n}\bigl(O_{a,i}/\bar O_a-O_{r,i}/\bar O_r\bigr)^2$ \Comment{$n$ times the variance of $\log\widehat\vartheta_n$}
  \State $h_n\gets\widehat\sigma_n\,(n^\ast)^{1/2}\,h(n/n^\ast)/n$ \Comment{normal-mixture radius}
  \State $C_n\gets\bigl[\log\widehat\vartheta_n-\log\lambda_+-h_n,\ \log\widehat\vartheta_n-\log\lambda_-+h_n\bigr]$
  \If{$w>b$} $\mathrm{LB}\gets\max(\mathrm{LB},\min C_n)$;\quad $\mathrm{UB}\gets\min(\mathrm{UB},\max C_n)$ \EndIf \Comment{burn-in}
  \If{$\mathrm{LB}>\log\delta$ \textbf{or} $\mathrm{UB}<-\log\delta$ \textbf{or} $-\log\delta<\mathrm{LB}\le\mathrm{UB}<\log\delta$} \textbf{stop} \Comment{S1--S2}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $[\mathrm{LB},\mathrm{UB}]$ for $\log\mathrm{AR}(d)$ and the conclusion of \cref{tab:prot-decisions}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\Cref{alg:sequential} is the delta-method confidence sequence of \citet{waudbysmith2024asymp} for the log of a ratio of two means, widened by the range for $\lambda$. The running intersection $[\mathrm{LB},\mathrm{UB}]$ uses only the sets after the burn-in, and the protocol registers $b=1$: the first wave updates the estimates but can neither narrow $[\mathrm{LB},\mathrm{UB}]$ nor stop the audit. The tuning constant is fixed before the audit as $a=-2\log\alpha+\log(1-2\log\alpha)$, which nearly minimises the radius at the planned number of pairs $n^\ast$. The protocol runs one copy per persona, at level $\alpha/|\mathcal D_A|$, and the same construction applied to the pair differences $O_{a,i}-O_{r,i}$ gives a confidence sequence for $\mathrm{AA}(d)$ when $\lambda=1$.

\begin{theorem}[Validity of \cref{alg:sequential}]\label{thm:sequential}
Let \cref{asm:id-nointerf}, \cref{asm:id-stable}(a) and \cref{asm:id-script} hold, so that the pairs $(O_{a,i},O_{r,i})$ are independent and identically distributed. Let $\E O_a,\E O_r>0$, $\lambda=\vartheta/\mathrm{AR}\in[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$, and let $\psi_i=O_{a,i}/\E O_a-O_{r,i}/\E O_r$ have variance $\sigma^2_\psi\in(0,\infty)$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Approximate time-uniform coverage.} Let the burn-in end after $n_{\mathrm b}=\lceil cn^\ast\rceil$ pairs, for a fixed $c>0$. Then, as the planned number of pairs $n^\ast\to\infty$,
  \[
    \limsup_{n^\ast\to\infty}\Pr(\exists\,n\ge n_{\mathrm b}:\log\mathrm{AR}\notin C_n)\le\bar\alpha(c)=\Pr\bigl(\exists\,s\ge c:|\mathbb B_s|\ge h(s)\bigr)\le\alpha,
  \]
  where $\mathbb B$ is a standard Brownian motion. Moreover $\bar\alpha(c)\to\alpha$ as $c\to0$, so the bound is not vacuous. Consequently, the probability that the audit misses $\log\mathrm{AR}$ at a random number of pairs $N\ge n_{\mathrm b}$, stopping or not, and the probability that rule S1 or S2 states a conclusion that the true $\mathrm{AR}$ contradicts, are at most $\bar\alpha(c)+o(1)$.
  \item \emph{Exact variant.} Let $\mathrm{Cap}_n(\vartheta)$ be the hedged capital process of \citet{waudbysmith2024betting}, with predictable bets, for the null hypothesis that $O_{a,i}-\vartheta O_{r,i}\in[-\vartheta,1]$ has mean zero. Then $C^{\mathrm{bet}}_n=\{\log\vartheta-\log\lambda':\max_{n'\le n}\mathrm{Cap}_{n'}(\vartheta)<1/\alpha,\ \lambda'\in[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]\}$ satisfies $\Pr(\exists\,n\ge1:\log\mathrm{AR}\notin C^{\mathrm{bet}}_n)\le\alpha$ for every sample size, without asymptotics.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) Since $\lambda\in[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$, $\log\mathrm{AR}\notin C_n$ implies $|\log\widehat\vartheta_n-\log\vartheta|>h_n$. By the delta method and the law of the iterated logarithm, $n(\log\widehat\vartheta_n-\log\vartheta)=\sum_{i\le n}\psi_i+O(\log\log n)$ almost surely. Because $O_u$ is bounded, the strong approximation of \citet{komlos1975,komlos1976} couples $\sum_{i\le n}\psi_i$ with $\sigma_\psi\mathbb W(n)$ for a Brownian motion $\mathbb W$, with an error of order $\log n$, which is negligible against the boundary for $n\ge cn^\ast$. Brownian scaling turns the boundary into $h$, and Ville's inequality applied to the normal mixture of exponential martingales \citep{robbins1970} bounds the crossing probability by $\alpha$. Tying the mixture to $n^\ast$ matters: with a fixed mixture, the limit over late times is zero for every $\alpha$ and would carry no information about the level. (b) Under the null, the hedged capital process is a nonnegative martingale with initial value one, and Ville's inequality applies directly. Details are in \cref{app:pf-sequential}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What is exact and what is approximate}
We state the approximations plainly.
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
  \item Part (a) is a large-sample statement, with the planned horizon and the burn-in growing together, so the guarantee is approximate at the protocol's sizes. The normal approximation is poorest in the first wave, when only a few pairs are available and the outcome is skewed. That is why one wave of burn-in ($b=1$) is registered. Even with it, at five pairs per wave the miss rate exceeds its nominal level at $\alpha=0.0125$ (coverage $98.0$--$98.8\%$ against $98.75\%$), though not at $\alpha=0.05$ ($95.0$--$96.2\%$) (\cref{sec:sim-sequential}). If the time-uniform coverage falls below the nominal level at the registered sizes, the exact sequence of part (b) is the fallback.
  \item The double-sampled outcome is harder for the normal approximation than the confirmed-flagged one. At the protocol's sizes each coded unflagged position stands for about $16$ recorded positions, so a single false negative found among the codes adds about $0.04$ to $\widetilde E_u$, against a mean exposure near $0.02$: the distribution of $\widetilde E_u$ is heavy-tailed, and a few puppets can dominate a wave. % ALIGN-WITH-SIM: replace the next sentence with the E5b result (coverage of Alg. 1 on \widetilde E_u at the registered design, AR and AA sequences, null/ND/DD).
  Whether \cref{alg:sequential} keeps its level on $\widetilde E_u$ at the registered design is the subject of experiment E5b (\cref{sec:sim-sequential}).
  \item For the confirmed-flagged outcome, false negatives enter only through $\lambda_S$; a confidence sequence for $\lambda_S$ at level $\alpha_v$ from coded unflagged positions can replace the declared range, at level $\alpha+\alpha_v$.
  \item Under drift, a time-uniform central limit theorem of Lindeberg type \citep{waudbysmith2024asymp} makes the sequence target the ratio of window-averaged means, $\lambda\overline{\mathrm{AR}}$ if $\lambda$ does not drift, and nothing beyond that.
  \item The exact variant (b) pays for the range of its observations: $O_u$ can lie anywhere in $[0,1]$ while its typical value is near $0.02$, so the capital grows slowly. It is a robustness check.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Peeking}
For comparison, consider the analyst who recomputes a fixed-sample $95\%$ interval after every wave and stops when it excludes the null. By the law of the iterated logarithm, the standardised estimate leaves $[-1.96,1.96]$ with probability one if monitoring continues indefinitely, and with eight equally spaced looks at a Gaussian random walk the probability that some look rejects is $17.6\%$ rather than $5\%$ \citep{armitage1969}. \Cref{alg:sequential} pays for the right to look by a wider radius, quantified in \cref{prop:power}(c). \Cref{sec:simulation} compares the two on the synthetic recommender.

\subsection{Power and sample size}\label{sec:meas-power}

The design question is how many puppets, sessions per puppet and codes are needed to conclude that $\mathrm{AR}=r_1$ lies outside the null region bounded by $r_0$. For detection, $r_0=1$; for the materiality rule S1, $r_0=\delta$; and the registered alternative is $r_1=\delta^2$ in both cases (\cref{sec:prot-stopping}). Let $n$ be the number of puppets per arm, which is also the number of pairs, and let $\sigma^2_{\mathrm s,\pi}$ and $\gamma_\pi$ be the variance and the intra-puppet correlation of the \emph{true} session exposures. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pw-var}
  \sigma^2_{E,\pi}=\frac{\sigma^2_{\mathrm s,\pi}\{1+(S-1)\gamma_\pi\}}{S},\qquad
  \sigma^2_{\mathrm c,\pi}=\frac{\varsigma^2_\pi}{kTS},\qquad
  \varsigma^2_\pi=\mu_\pi\Se(1-\Se)+(1-\mu_\pi)\Sp(1-\Sp).
\end{equation}
The first is the variance of a puppet's true exposure, with the design effect of \citet{kish1965}. The second is the variance that the classifier adds to $\bar Q_u$ when its errors are independent across positions given the labels, and $\varsigma^2_\pi$ is the corresponding variance per position. Let
\[
  V=\sum_\pi\frac{\sigma^2_{E,\pi}+\sigma^2_{\mathrm c,\pi}/J^2}{\mu_\pi^2},\qquad
  V_0=\sum_\pi\frac{\sigma^2_{E,\pi}}{\mu_\pi^2},
\]
let $\Delta=|\log r_1-\log r_0|$, and let $z=z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}$. The validation term depends on the design. With error rates pooled over the arms (external design under \cref{asm:id-nondiff}), with error rates stratified by arm (external design per arm, $n_{1\pi}$ positives and $n_{0\pi}$ negatives), and with internal double sampling ($m_{1\pi}$ flagged and $m_{0\pi}$ unflagged codes per arm), it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pw-B}
\begin{aligned}
  V_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{pool}}&=\frac{(1/\mu_a-1/\mu_r)^2\,\Sp(1-\Sp)}{J^2n_0},\qquad
  V_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{st}}=\sum_\pi\frac{1}{J_\pi^2}\Bigl\{\frac{\Se_\pi(1-\Se_\pi)}{n_{1\pi}}+\Bigl(\frac{1-\mu_\pi}{\mu_\pi}\Bigr)^2\frac{\Sp_\pi(1-\Sp_\pi)}{n_{0\pi}}\Bigr\},\\
  V_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{ds}}&=\sum_\pi\frac{1}{\mu_\pi^2}\Bigl\{\frac{q_\pi^2\PPV_\pi(1-\PPV_\pi)}{m_{1\pi}}+\frac{(1-q_\pi)^2\FOR_\pi(1-\FOR_\pi)}{m_{0\pi}}\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In $V_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{pool}}$ the specificity enters through the difference $1/\mu_a-1/\mu_r$, because a common error in $\widehat\Sp$ shifts both arms. In $V_{\mathrm{val}}^{\mathrm{st}}$ the two arms' errors are independent and nothing cancels.

\begin{proposition}[Power and sample size]\label{prop:power}
Let $\log\mathrm{AR}=\log r_1$, with $n$ puppets per arm and $S$ sessions per puppet.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Fixed sample.} Under the conditions of \cref{thm:misclass}(b) or (d), with $V_{\mathrm{val}}$ the validation term \eqref{eq:pw-B} of the design used, the normal approximation to the probability that the interval for $\log\mathrm{AR}$ excludes $\log r_0$ is at least $1-\beta$ if and only if
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:pw-n}
    n\ \ge\ n_{\mathrm{fix}}=\frac{z^2V}{\Delta^2-z^2V_{\mathrm{val}}},\qquad\text{which requires}\qquad \Delta^2>z^2V_{\mathrm{val}}.
  \end{equation}
  With the other stratum of validation unlimited and equal sizes in the two arms, the requirement is $n_0>n_0^{\min}$, $n_{0\pi}>n_{0,\mathrm{st}}^{\min}$ or $m_{0\pi}>m_0^{\min}$, where
  \begin{gather*}
    n_0^{\min}=\frac{z^2(1/\mu_a-1/\mu_r)^2\Sp(1-\Sp)}{J^2\Delta^2},\qquad
    n_{0,\mathrm{st}}^{\min}=\frac{z^2}{\Delta^2}\sum_\pi\Bigl(\frac{1-\mu_\pi}{\mu_\pi}\Bigr)^2\frac{\Sp_\pi(1-\Sp_\pi)}{J_\pi^2},\\
    m_0^{\min}=\frac{z^2}{\Delta^2}\sum_\pi\frac{(1-q_\pi)^2\FOR_\pi(1-\FOR_\pi)}{\mu_\pi^2}.
  \end{gather*}
  Below these sizes no number of puppets achieves the target power. The ratio $V/V_0$ is the variance inflation due to classifier noise, and $V_{\mathrm{val}}$ is the inflation due to validation. For double sampling, the factor $J_\pi^{-2}$ in the classifier part of $V$ becomes $(\PPV_\pi-\FOR_\pi)^2$; we write $V^{\mathrm{ds}}$ for the result.
  \item \emph{Sessions per puppet.} Write $V=v_{\mathrm p}+v_{\mathrm s}/S$, with $v_{\mathrm p}=\sum_\pi\sigma^2_{\mathrm s,\pi}\gamma_\pi/\mu_\pi^2$ and $v_{\mathrm s}=\sum_\pi\{\sigma^2_{\mathrm s,\pi}(1-\gamma_\pi)+\varsigma^2_\pi/(kTJ^2)\}/\mu_\pi^2$. If a puppet costs $c_p$ and each of its sessions $c_s$, the cost $n(c_p+Sc_s)$ of meeting \eqref{eq:pw-n} with $V_{\mathrm{val}}=0$ is minimised at $S^\ast=\{(c_p/c_s)\,v_{\mathrm s}/v_{\mathrm p}\}^{1/2}$.
  \item \emph{Sequential.} For a normal-mixture confidence sequence tuned to $n^\ast$ pairs, the radius at $n^\ast$ is $c_\alpha(V/n^\ast)^{1/2}$, where
  \[
    c_\alpha=h(1)=\bigl\{2(1+a^{-1})\log\bigl((1+a)^{1/2}/\alpha\bigr)\bigr\}^{1/2}
  \]
  does not depend on $n^\ast$. A horizon of $n_{\mathrm{seq}}=(c_\alpha+z_{1-\beta})^2V/\Delta^2$ pairs gives power at least $1-\beta$ by the horizon, to the same approximation, and the expected number of pairs used is smaller because the audit can stop early.
  \item \emph{Confirmed-flagged outcome.} For $O_u=Y_u$ with $m$ codes per puppet, $V$ is replaced by
  \[
    V_Y=\sum_\pi\frac{1}{\mu_\pi^2}\Bigl\{\sigma^2_{E,\pi}+\frac{\mu_\pi(1-\Se_\pi)}{\Se_\pi kTS}+\frac{q_\pi^2\PPV_\pi(1-\PPV_\pi)}{m\,\Se_\pi^2}\Bigr\},
  \]
  up to the finite-population correction of the coding and the variation of $\PPV$ across puppets. With the declared range $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$, the horizon becomes $n_{\mathrm{seq}}=(c_\alpha+z_{1-\beta})^2V_Y/\{\Delta-\log(\lambda_+/\lambda_-)\}^2$, which requires $\Delta>\log(\lambda_+/\lambda_-)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) $\log\widehat{\mathrm{AR}}$ is approximately normal with mean $\log r_1$ and variance $V/n+V_{\mathrm{val}}$, by \cref{prop:mc-variance} and the law of total variance for $\bar Q_u$ given the labels; setting the power $\Phi\{\Delta(V/n+V_{\mathrm{val}})^{-1/2}-z_{1-\alpha/2}\}$ to $1-\beta$ gives \eqref{eq:pw-n}. (b) Minimise $(v_{\mathrm p}+v_{\mathrm s}/S)(c_p+Sc_s)$ over $S>0$. (c) Substitute the tuning into the radius. (d) Condition on the record and then on the labels; in the worst case allowed by the range, the estimated log ratio is shifted by $\log(\lambda_+/\lambda_-)$ towards the null. Details are in \cref{app:pf-power}.
\end{proof}

For $\alpha=0.05$, $c_\alpha=3.04$, against $z_{0.975}=1.96$. For the per-persona level $\alpha=0.0125$ of a four-persona audit, $c_\alpha=3.50$, against $z_{0.99375}=2.50$. Being allowed to look after every wave therefore costs a horizon $\{(c_\alpha+z_{1-\beta})/(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})\}^2$ times the fixed sample size, which is $1.9$ at $\alpha=0.05$ and $1.7$ at $\alpha=0.0125$ for power $0.8$. Early stopping recovers part of this, and \cref{sec:simulation} reports the distribution of stopping times.

\Cref{tab:prot-power} in \cref{sec:prot-power} applies \cref{prop:power} to invented but plausible inputs at the protocol's design. Three conclusions hold across its rows. First, materiality is expensive: with four personas it needs about five times as many puppets as detection, because $\Delta$ falls from $\log1.5625$ to $\log1.25$ and the per-persona level is stricter, and its horizon, about $610$ pairs per persona, is an order of magnitude beyond a conventional small-lab design with $40$ puppets per cell over eight waves of five pairs. Second, validation can bind before puppets do: with error rates stratified by arm, as protection against differential error requires, about $21{,}400$ coded negatives are needed per arm and persona before any number of puppets suffices, whereas double sampling's coding grows with the number of puppets, and the confirmed-flagged outcome replaces the floor by the range for $\lambda_S$, a range of $\pm5\%$ more than tripling its four-persona horizon. Third, the optimal number of sessions per puppet is small, $S^\ast\approx3$--$7$, because sessions of one puppet are correlated, and fewer sessions also mean a smaller footprint.
