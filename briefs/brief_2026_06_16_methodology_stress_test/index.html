Where Campus Evidence Lab Can Be Wrong

This memo describes ways Campus Evidence Lab can be wrong, incomplete, skewed, or misused. It is meant to make the archive more reviewable, not to defend every record as complete or final.

Analysis Notes

  • Public-source bias means the archive sees what becomes public and may miss private reports, sealed records, unreported incidents, and lived experience.
  • Discoverability bias means records are easier to include when sources are findable, stable, indexed, and clearly written.
  • School size, reporting practice, agency activity, and source-type concentration can shape counts without measuring underlying prevalence.
  • Legal/procedural ambiguity and affected-community label ambiguity require reviewers to track source wording and avoid unsupported inference.

Responsible Uses

  • Use this memo to find weak assumptions before citing records or briefs.
  • Pair count-based analysis with coverage limits and source-type context.
  • Treat risk controls as review aids, not proof that every record is complete or final.

Method Note
