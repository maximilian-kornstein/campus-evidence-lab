Why Public Records Are Not School Rankings

Campus Evidence Lab added an analysis memo explaining why the 4,000-record archive should be read as public documentation infrastructure, not as a ranking system, safety score, severity score, or legal conclusion.

Analysis Notes

  • Record counts are shaped by public-source availability, source discovery, reporting practices, school size, jurisdiction, institutional transparency, and reviewer capacity.
  • A high count can mean more documentation, not necessarily more underlying incidents.
  • A low count can mean fewer discoverable public records, not necessarily fewer incidents or stronger institutional conditions.
  • Responsible comparisons should describe documentation patterns and source coverage rather than campus safety or quality of life.

Responsible Uses

  • Cite the snapshot and source pages when discussing record counts.
  • Use counts to identify documentation gaps, not to label schools.
  • Pair any quantitative observation with the methodology limitations.

Method Note

This memo uses the current event, source, school, and snapshot artifacts. It adds no new event records and makes no prevalence claim.
