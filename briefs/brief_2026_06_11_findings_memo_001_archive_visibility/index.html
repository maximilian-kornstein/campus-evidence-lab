Findings Memo 001: What The Archive Makes Visible

Campus Evidence Lab published a reporter-facing findings memo explaining what the 4,000-record archive currently makes visible, which source families dominate the public record, and why those patterns should be read as documentation structure rather than campus rankings or prevalence estimates.

Analysis Notes

  • The current archive is dominated by two event categories: Harassment or threat (2,196 records) and Vandalism (1,453 records). This means the archive is strongest as a record of public documentation categories, not as a balanced portrait of every form of campus civil-rights harm.
  • Affected-community labels are also concentrated: Race (1,287 records), LGBTQ+ (1,005), and Religion (754) are the largest labels in the current snapshot. Those counts reflect what the current source mix captures and classifies, not what is most common across campuses.
  • Source-type concentration is highly visible: Government dataset (3,845 records) drives most of the current archive, while Government release (96) and Annual security report (46) provide much smaller layers of public detail. That concentration helps with reproducibility but limits narrative depth.
  • The strongest inference available from the current snapshot is where public documentation is thick, thin, or standardized. The archive does not support school rankings, safety scores, severity scores, or incident prevalence claims.

Responsible Uses

  • Use the archive to identify public record patterns, reporting leads, and source gaps.
  • Pair any quantitative statement with the methodology and research-guide limits.
  • Use the linked source page and source URL before making institution-specific factual claims.

Method Note

This memo uses the current public snapshot only. It does not add records, update records, or claim that documentation counts measure underlying incident prevalence.
