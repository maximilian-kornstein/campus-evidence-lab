What This Makes Possible

Public-use surface

Searchable memory

Scattered reports become event pages, school timelines, source pages, CSV exports, JSON exports, and research briefs.

Reviewable claims

Every public record points to source material, confidence labels, verification status, and deterministic record hashes.

Free contribution path

Source submissions, corrections, duplicate reports, and metadata fixes can be prepared without accounts or paid infrastructure.

Institutional continuity

Snapshots, changelogs, release notes, and source audits preserve what changed and when it changed.

Current Use Cases

Useful without overclaiming

Journalists

Find public-source records, source pages, and school timelines before requesting comment or records from institutions. The press brief and journalist guide define the reporting surface.

Student leaders

Compare source-backed documentation patterns and identify where public records are thin, stale, or hard to audit.

Civil-rights researchers

Use hashes, source audits, filtered exports, the research workspace, and methodology limits to build reproducible questions from public evidence.

Prospective students

Read source material and institutional responses without treating record counts as rankings, safety scores, or legal findings.

Public Roadmap

Conservative sequence

Phase 1
Keep the 4,000-record archive stable, searchable, auditable, and correction-friendly.
Phase 2
Recruit a small reviewer circle to audit methodology, source categories, duplicate handling, and neutral language.
Phase 3
Publish partner-reviewed research briefs only after outside review creates documented value.
Phase 4
Explore institutional transparency tools after the evidence standards and reviewer workflow are proven.

Claims Not Made

Limits matter

  • The dataset is not a complete census of campus hate or discrimination incidents.
  • Record counts are not school rankings, safety scores, or severity scores.
  • Absence from the dataset does not mean absence of incidents, complaints, or institutional responses.
  • Confidence labels describe source support, not moral seriousness or legal truth.
